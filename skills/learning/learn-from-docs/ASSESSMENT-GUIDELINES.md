# Assessment Guidelines

Assessment establishes what the learner can explain, apply, or critically evaluate from the agreed source scope. It is a feedback loop, not a grade or an interrogation.

## Initial orientation

- Before the first section, ask two to four questions or use one tiny application task only when it will change the study path.
- Probe source-relevant prior knowledge, related reading, terminology, and likely misconceptions; do not test general trivia.
- Record the resulting baseline in `PROGRESS.md`. Write a learning record when stated prior knowledge materially changes future study.

## Evidence and feedback

- Prefer observable evidence: a correct explanation in the learner's own words, comparison of claims, application to a scenario, critique of a limitation, or a completed task.
- Give feedback promptly, identify the relevant source concept or section, and offer the smallest useful corrective action.
- Use `introduced` for material encountered, `practiced` for a guided or corrected attempt, and `demonstrated` for an appropriate successful explanation or application.
- Reading a section, receiving notes, or repeating a quotation is not demonstration.
- Write a learning record when demonstrated understanding, a corrected misconception, or stated prior knowledge meaningfully changes the remaining study path.

## Completion and synthesis

- Derive the closing assessment from the `SOURCE.md` success criteria and agreed scope.
- Prefer a small, authentic outcome: explain an argument, compare approaches, apply a method to a scenario, or identify a source limitation.
- End with short retrieval review plus the synthesis; if evidence is partial, keep the remaining takeaway and next step in `PROGRESS.md` rather than declaring completion.
