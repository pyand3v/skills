# Assessment Guidelines

Assessment establishes what the learner can do and what should come next. It is a feedback loop, not a grade or an interrogation. Keep it tied to the mission and proportionate to the learner's time budget.

## Initial diagnosis

- Before the first lesson, use two to four targeted questions or one tiny practical task.
- Probe the concepts and tasks most relevant to the mission, not general trivia.
- Start with a plausible level of challenge, then adjust after the learner's response rather than testing every prerequisite.
- Record the resulting baseline and any stated prior knowledge in `PROGRESS.md`; use a learning record when that knowledge materially changes future teaching.

## Evidence and feedback

- Prefer observable evidence: a correct explanation, successful application, completed exercise, reviewed work, or observed outcome.
- Give feedback immediately, explain a misconception when needed, and offer the smallest useful corrective action.
- Apply the `introduced`, `practiced`, and `demonstrated` definitions in `PROGRESS-FORMAT.md`. Exposure to a lesson alone is not evidence of capability.
- Write a learning record when a demonstrated capability, corrected misconception, or stated prior knowledge will meaningfully alter future sessions.

## Capstone and completion

- Derive the capstone or application task from one or more observable `MISSION.md` success criteria.
- Prefer a small, real outcome over a synthetic comprehensive exam: for example, a completed performance, useful artifact, solved problem, reviewed work, or explained decision.
- End with a short retrieval review alongside the capstone so the learner can both recall and apply the capability.
- If the evidence is partial, do not declare the mission complete; update `PROGRESS.md` with the remaining capability and most useful next step.
